Money vs. Movers

Migration Analysis — U-Haul Growth Index vs. IRS Wealth Migration
01

The Divergence

Texas has topped U-Haul's Growth Index 7 of the last 10 years. But the IRS tells a different story.

Florida captured $20.65B in net adjusted gross income in 2023. Texas: $5.5B. That's roughly $3.75 of net wealth flowing into Florida for every $1 into Texas — despite Texas leading in raw household count.

One is a volume story. The other is a wealth story. Hustlers vs. retirees. They underwrite very differently.

Source: U-Haul Growth Index 2025, IRS SOI Migration Data 2022–2023.
02

Dollars per Door

Strip away total volume and ask: what does the average inbound household actually earn?

  • Florida: $163K per net inbound household
  • South Carolina: $108K
  • Texas: $66K

The US median household income is ~$75K. Texas is pulling movers below that line. Florida and South Carolina are pulling movers well above it.

Same Sun Belt. Very different tenant profiles. Very different rent ceilings.

Source: IRS SOI Migration Data 2022–2023.
03

Flow of Capital, per Mover

Florida captures $34,100 in net AGI for every person who moves in. Texas: $10,000. That puts Texas in the same tier as Idaho and Nevada — not leading the pack.

On the other end: New York loses $36.9K per mover. California: $30.6K. Massachusetts: $34.4K. The people arriving in those states bring materially less income than the people leaving.

The second tier is worth watching. New Hampshire, Maine, Delaware, Idaho, and Montana are all capturing $14K–$19K per mover without ever making U-Haul's top 10. Selective wealth capture, not volume.

Source: IRS SOI State Migration Data 2022–2023.
04

The Austin Split

Zoom from state to county and the finding flips.

Within the Austin MSA, wealth migration is a suburban story:

  • Hays County: +$19.8K per mover (higher than Florida's state average)
  • Williamson County: +$17.1K per mover
  • Travis County: −$1.8K per mover

Travis isn't declining — it's sorting. High earners are moving to Leander, Lakeway, Dripping Springs, Kyle, and Buda. That shows up as Travis outflow and Williamson/Hays inflow.

For context: Dallas is at −$10.6K per mover. Harris (Houston) is at −$7.8K. The same suburban wealth migration is happening across Texas metros.

The Austin MSA weighted average is still +$8.3K — the suburbs are doing the heavy lifting.

Source: IRS SOI County Migration Data 2022–2023.
