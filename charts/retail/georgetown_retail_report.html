Summary

  • Georgetown added 2,427,632 SF of retail space from 2018 Q4 to 2026 Q2.
  • This is 33% of all new retail space in the Austin metro.
  • Georgetown holds only 9.8% of the metro retail inventory.
  • The Georgetown vacancy rate decreased from 4.16% to 3.49% in the same period.
  • The pipeline is now 36.9% of the existing Georgetown inventory.
Read this first
  • The CoStar “Georgetown” submarket is north Williamson County. It is not the city.
  • The city of Georgetown has 49.9% of the submarket inventory.
  • Other cities in the submarket are Taylor 12.4%, Round Rock 9.6%, Hutto 8.3%, Leander 7.6%, Liberty Hill 5.8% and Jarrell 4.7%.
  • All data in this report is for the full submarket.

Supply

Georgetown built more space than any other submarket

  • Georgetown added 2,427,632 SF. The other 15 submarkets added 4,958,706 SF in total.
  • Georgetown inventory increased 33.7%. The rest of Austin increased 5.9%.
  • Six submarkets decreased in size during this period.
  • In 2025 Georgetown delivered 644,272 SF. This is 45.7% of the metro total for that year.
  • The 2025 total is more than two times the Georgetown annual average from 2019 to 2024.

Absorption

Absorption matched supply until 2025

  • Georgetown absorbed 2,391,098 SF against 2,427,632 SF of new supply. The capture rate is 98.5%.
  • The vacancy rate decreased from 4.16% to 3.49% during the build-out.
  • The rest of Austin has a capture rate of 112.7%. These submarkets built very little space. They filled empty space that already existed.
  • The Georgetown rate is more difficult to achieve. Georgetown absorbed new space, not existing empty space.
  • In 2025 supply was 644,272 SF. Absorption was 533,109 SF. The vacancy rate increased from 3.2% to 4.2%.One year is not a trend. In 2026 Q2 the vacancy rate decreased again to 3.5%.

Metro context

Inventory growth by submarket

  • Georgetown has the highest growth rate of all submarkets at 33.7%.
  • Far Northeast is second at 24.7%. Cedar Park is third at 19.8%.
  • The submarkets at the edge of the metro grew. The submarkets in the urban core decreased.
  • Six submarkets lost inventory. Southeast decreased 11.3%. CBD decreased 6.3%. Central decreased 5.1%.

West corridor

  • Growth since 2018 is 73.2%. This is the highest of the three corridors.
  • The corridor added 32.4% more space in the last three years. It absorbed almost all of that space.
  • The pipeline is 96.8% of the existing inventory. The corridor can almost double in size.

Center corridor

  • The corridor holds 63.4% of the submarket inventory.
  • The vacancy rate is 3.1%. This is the lowest of the three corridors.
  • The NNN rent is $27.60. This is the highest of the three corridors.
  • The pipeline is 21.6% of the existing inventory.

East corridor

  • The corridor added only 3.4% more space in the last three years.
  • The vacancy rate decreased from 7.5% to 4.6%. The corridor filled existing empty space.
  • The NNN rent is $24.60. This is the lowest of the three corridors.
  • The pipeline is 33.8% of the existing inventory. The Samsung Taylor development is a probable cause.

Pipeline

The pipeline is 36.9% of existing inventory

  • Georgetown has 3,556,909 SF under construction or proposed.
  • Under construction: 521,706 SF in 35 buildings.
  • Proposed: 3,035,203 SF in 107 projects.
  • The pipeline is approximately 39% of the full metro retail pipeline.
  • The Georgetown absorption rate is 371,606 SF each year. At this rate the market needs 9.6 years to absorb the pipeline.
  • This ratio is the highest in the metro for a submarket with significant absorption.
  • A proposed project has no delivery commitment. Many proposed projects do not start construction.
What to watch
  • Two adjacent parcels on Rockride Lane hold 646,053 SF of proposed space.
  • This is more than Georgetown absorbed in all of 2025.
  • The delivery dates of these two parcels control the vacancy rate more than the other 105 proposed projects.

Method

Data and calculation method

  • The data source is CoStar inventory and CoStar quarterly statistics.
  • The data includes existing retail buildings only.
  • Occupied SF = rentable area − direct vacant SF − sublet vacant SF.
  • Net absorption = the change in occupied SF from one quarter to the next quarter.
  • Net new supply = the change in rentable area of the existing stock. This value includes demolitions and reclassifications.
  • The report starts at 2018 Q4. CoStar increased panel coverage in 2018 Q3 from approximately 2,000 buildings to more than 8,700 buildings.
  • Earlier quarters are not comparable.
  • This report uses ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English.
Caveats
  • The 2026 data is year-to-date through Q2. Do not multiply it to get an annual value.
  • The rest-of-Austin supply is negative in 2026. CoStar decreased the rentable area of 134 buildings.
  • The largest correction is The Landing at ABIA. CoStar decreased this building by 609,689 SF.
  • These changes are corrections. They are not demolitions. Use the 2026 metro values with caution.
  • Rent values are RBA-weighted NNN direct asking rents for the latest quarter.
