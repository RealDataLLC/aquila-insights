# 2026 Recap: 5 Most Significant Industrial Sales in Austin, Texas

*Draft, 2026-11-16. Covers sales through AQUILA's 3Q 2026 Quarterly Report. Refresh against the 4Q 2026 report in January. Multi-building trades are combined and ranked by total square footage.*

Institutional capital came back to Austin industrial in 2026, and it came back in bulk. The year's defining trend was Link Logistics, Blackstone's industrial platform, selling much of its Austin infill holdings to several buyers. Meanwhile, a public REIT paid more than $200/SF for newer product in Northeast Austin, and an Austin space company bought its own campus.

## 1. Link Parks Portfolio: BKM Capital Partners / Kayne Anderson Real Estate

**About 1.37 million SF across 22 industrial and flex buildings (2Q 2026).** BKM Capital Partners and Kayne Anderson Real Estate bought a 22-building package from Link Logistics Real Estate. It spans the McNeil, McKalla, Rutland, Braker, Waterford and Southpark parks in North Central and Southeast Austin. The largest building is McNeil 1 at 246,592 SF. The portfolio also included the 57,165 SF Waterford C office building. It was the largest industrial trade in the market this year.

## 2. Corridor Park: Stonemont Financial Group / PCCP

**451,555 SF across six buildings (3Q 2026).** Stonemont and PCCP bought all six buildings at Corridor Park in North Central Austin from Link Logistics. Buildings range from 57,600 SF to 96,000 SF.

## 3. Harris Ridge Business Center: EastGroup Properties

**388,000 SF across five buildings, about $83.0 million, roughly $214/SF (3Q 2026).** EastGroup Properties (NYSE: EGP) bought Buildings 1, 3, 4, 5 and 6 at Harris Ridge in Northeast Austin from TA Realty. Building 5, at 155,600 SF, accounted for about $34.9 million. This was the year's clearest public pricing benchmark for newer multi-tenant Austin industrial.

## 4. West Austin Business Park: CesiumAstro

**269,959 SF across three buildings (1Q 2026).** CesiumAstro, the Austin-based satellite and phased-array communications company, bought Buildings 1–3 at West Austin Business Park from Velocis and KBC Advisors. It was the year's largest owner-user purchase and another sign of how fast Austin's aerospace and defense cluster is growing.

## 5. Austin Shared Logistics: Transport Properties

**166,310 SF across two buildings (2Q 2026).** Transport Properties bought Austin Shared Logistics in Northeast Austin from Southern Mail Service.

## Also notable

- **Northgate 35 Logistics Center** (145,454 SF, Northeast): The Stone Collection bought it from BGRE, another owner-user purchase.
- **210 E. Saint Elmo Rd.** (102,934 SF, South): WareSpace bought it from Link Logistics as part of a portfolio.
- **Southpark D** (75,525 SF, Southeast): Leifer Properties bought it from Pennybacker.

## What it means

Link Logistics alone sold nearly 2 million SF of Austin industrial in 2026, to three different buyer groups. The buyers were mostly private equity and REIT capital betting on infill North Central and Southeast Austin locations, where new supply is limited. Owner-users like CesiumAstro and The Stone Collection were the other active buyers. They chose to own rather than lease in a market with plenty of options.

*Source: AQUILA Quarterly Report sales grids (1Q–3Q 2026), CoStar. Prices are shown only where publicly reported.*
